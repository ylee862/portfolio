Fitoday

A social fashion app where you log what you wear each day, level up, and grow your style with feedback from the community.

Role
Solo: research, product, UI, build
Timeline
June 2026 – ongoing
Platform
iOS, Expo (React Native) + Supabase
Tools
Figma, Claude Code
Status
V3 prototype in user testing

Problem

People want honest feedback on their outfits, and an easy way to keep track of what they wear.

Solution

A daily outfit log. Log what you wore, come back to level up, and rate and give feedback to each other to grow your style.

The pivot

V2 was built on ratings, so new users with few connections got nothing back. V3 keeps every feature but makes logging the core.

Problem

Fashion apps are built for sharing outfits, not for improving them.

It started with a personal question. Scrolling Instagram, I kept wondering why some outfits looked put together and mine didn't. What I wanted wasn't more inspiration. It was honest feedback I could learn from.

To check whether that was just me, I ran a 12-person survey and reviewed the platforms people already use for fashion. The pattern was clear: there are plenty of places to post an outfit and almost nowhere built for constructive feedback on it. People also told me why they don't ask for it: they're afraid of being judged.

  • I am scared that people will judge me. I don't think my fashion sense is good enough.Survey participant
  • I am not an influencer, but I still want to improve my style.Survey participant

Instagram, Pinterest and TikTok are built for showing outfits, not improving them.

V1–V2: A feedback app

Post one outfit a day. Others rate it in one tap.

I designed for two people: Chloe, who posts and wants better feedback than “nice pic”, and Marcus, who wants to dress better but is afraid to ask.

  • One outfit a dayThoughtful posts, a calm feed, a habit people can keep.
  • Three options, not likesImprove, Okay or Love it. Fast to tap, more useful than a like.
  • 24 hours per postInstead of a midnight cutoff that punishes late posters.

User testing

The rating worked. People wanted to say why, and a reason to come back.

Two rounds of think-aloud testing, V1 then V2, with the same five people.

  • Rating was instantly clearAll five understood it with no explanation.Kept as is.
  • A tap isn't enough“I want people to tell me where to improve.”Added an optional note, visible only to the poster.
  • No way to undoA participant mis-tapped and couldn't fix it.Ratings can be changed for 24 hours.
  • A reason to returnOne participant asked for a streak.Parked in the backlog. It later became the core of V3.

Market check

Users liked the interaction. But I hadn't planned for an app with few users.

Before writing code, I checked which outfit apps survive and where users leave.

  • Similar apps don't lastLookbook.nu went offline in 2023. Go Try It On reached 400,000 users and still shut down.
  • No plan for few usersA score needed 5 ratings. New users with no connections would post, get nothing back, and leave.

My mistake

I tested whether people liked the product, but not whether it would hold up with few users, or in the market.

Next time, I will ensure the market check comes before the first wireframe. However, as I caught it before code, the only cost was a PRD rewrite, not a rebuilt app.

The pivot

Same features, new core: give every user a reason to come back, even with no followers.

I asked what would keep a user who has no connections yet. My own habit answered: I took outfit photos every day, then lost them in my camera roll.

  • New coreDaily logging: log, look back, and level up with streaks.
  • Kept, already testedRatings, feedback notes and levels. Share an entry and the community helps you grow.
  • New primary userSam, a style tracker. Chloe moves to the community layer.

V3 prototype

Log every day, level up, and share for feedback when you want it.

  • Log in secondsA photo, then three taps or fewer.
  • Look backA month calendar, stats, and “last worn” search.
  • Pick an audienceOnly me, Followers or Public, per entry. Private by default.
  • Level upPoints for logging and streaks. Rating and giving feedback earn extra.

V3 user testing

The prototype is done. This time I want a bigger sample.

In progress

The feedback features were tested in V1–V2. Logging is the new core, so it needs its own round, with more than five people.

  • MethodA large group of AI-simulated users for breadth, plus a few real users to check the findings.
  • QuestionsDoes logging really take seconds? Would someone with no followers keep coming back?
  • ResultsComing soon.

Build plan

The stack and safety rules are set. Code starts once testing confirms the design.

  • StackExpo + Supabase
  • Safety firstSecurity and policy docs written before code: reporting, blocking, moderation, no tracking SDKs, birth date never stored.
  • ThenBuild, TestFlight, App Store.

Reflection

It's really hard to validate a product without a real market and I shouldn't assume it will work.

This project taught me that a product cannot be built in one sitting. Understanding the market and user needs is crucial before any development begins.
